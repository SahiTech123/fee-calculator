🛡️ How to Identify and Avoid Mobile Money Fraud in Tanzania

Date:

Introduction

Mobile money fraud is on the rise in Tanzania. Every day, many Tanzanians lose their hard-earned money to fraudsters who impersonate mobile network operators, banks, or even friends and family. The biggest problem is that most people don't know the signs of fraud and are easily tricked.

⚠️ Warning: Fraudsters use various tactics to steal your money. Be careful and never share your PIN or personal information with anyone.

Types of Mobile Money Fraud

1. "Company Representative" Fraud

Fraudsters call pretending to be representatives from M-Pesa, Tigo Pesa, or Airtel Money. They claim there is an issue with your account and ask for your PIN or phone number to "fix" it.

How to Protect Yourself: Mobile network companies never ask for your PIN over the phone. Never share your PIN or personal information with anyone.

2. "Prize Winner" Fraud

You receive a message saying you've won money (e.g., TZS 5,000,000). They ask you to send a "registration fee" or "tax fee" to claim your prize.

How to Protect Yourself: No legitimate competition will ask you to send money to claim a prize. This is a tactic used by fraudsters to steal your money.

3. "Fake Number" Fraud

Fraudsters use phone numbers that look similar to those of your friends or family. They send messages asking for financial help due to an emergency.

How to Protect Yourself: Before sending money, call the person first to verify it's really them.

4. "Suspicious Link" Fraud

You receive a message with a link that appears to be from a mobile network operator or bank. When you click it, you're asked to enter your phone number and PIN.

How to Protect Yourself: Do not click on links from people you don't know. Mobile network operators do not use links to ask for your personal information.

Signs of Fraud (Table)

How to Protect Yourself

💡 Key Rule: "No one can ask for your PIN". If someone asks for your PIN, it's fraud!

1. Never Share Your PIN

Your PIN is like your password. No person or company should ask for it. M-Pesa, Tigo Pesa, and Airtel Money will never ask for your PIN over the phone, SMS, or email.

2. Verify Before Sending Money

Before sending money to someone, call them first to confirm it's really them asking. Don't rely on messages alone.

3. Check the Phone Number

Before sending money, check the phone number using the Kikotoo cha Makato app. Our app has a list of numbers reported for fraud.

4. Report Fraud

If you encounter fraud, report it to your mobile network operator or the police. You can also add the number to our fraud list through the app.

5. Educate Others

Share this information with friends and family. Many people fall victim because they don't know the signs of fraud.

What to Do If You Are a Victim

  1. Don't panic - There are steps you can take.
  2. Contact your mobile network operator - M-Pesa (*150*00#), Tigo Pesa (*150*01#), Airtel Money (150).
  3. Report to the police - Visit the nearest police station.
  4. Report on our app - Add the fraudulent number to our list to help others.
⚠️ Remember: Never give your PIN or personal information to anyone. Mobile network operators will never ask for this information.

Conclusion

Mobile money fraud can be avoided by knowing the signs and being careful. By using the Kikotoo cha Makato app, you can check the safety of phone numbers and protect your money.
